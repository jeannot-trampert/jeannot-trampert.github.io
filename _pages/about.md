I am a Luxembourgish geophysicist. I studied mathematics, physics and later geophysics at Strasbourg University, where I also obtained my doctorate and habilitation. After my PhD, I was a postdoctoral scholar at Oxford University, before accepting a permanent post at Ecole et Observatoire des Sciences de la Terre in Strasbourg. Later, I moved to the Department of Earth Sciences at Utrecht University, where I stayed until now.

For about a century now, seismology has been a unique source of information on the (an)elastic and density structure of the Earth’s interior. A considerable difficulty is to relate the seismologically inferred structure to dynamical processes taking place within the Earth. During the last 20 years, in parallel with my continuing frontier work on refining and quantifying Earth’s structure, I have made a dedicated effort to constrain the dynamics of the Earth’s mantle and developed an integrated programme in seismology, mineral physics and later, in collaborative work, convection modelling. I made major contributions to the field of seismic tomography by introducing automatic surface wave dispersion measurements and the innovative concept of probabilistic tomography, crucial for combining information from different disciplines. I obtained paradigm-changing results on the distribution of temperature and composition in the lower mantle and established for the first time the presence of anisotropy in the mid-mantle transition zone, which put new constraints on the mineralogy and dynamics of the transition zone. I then turned my focus on full waveform inversion, where I made important technical contributions, and in parallel investigated how machine learning could be harnessed for solving suitable geophysical problems. Examples are work on the resolution analysis and misfit functions in full waveform inversion. Work on the deep structure of the North Atlantic revealed hitherto unknown flow patterns thus elucidating details of the process of opening of the northern Atlantic Ocean. My work on machine learning identified seamounts using autoencoders and exploited surface wave dispersion curves using Gaussian mixture networks. In general, my work is characterized by a synergy between observations and theoretically strong methodologies for quantitative analysis and interpretation. I also got interested in the physics of the seismic ruptures, where I developed a new approach for earthquake early warning based on neural networks. Recently, with a large interdisciplinary group, I started applying statistical physics techniques to observed scaling laws of earthquakes, establishing that earthquakes should in principle be predictable as they present long-range spatio-temporal correlations. I am now focussing on the elusive earthquake forecasting problem using symbolic regression to extract unknown physics from data.

All this work would not have been possible without dedicated and exceptionally gifted PhD candidates and post-doctoral scholars. Many of his former group members now occupy leading positions in industry and academia.
